# Planner visual system

Rules for every planner surface. The scales and primitives are in `design.css` (imported once from `plan.css`); the tone comes from the reading column on the product page: type and space do the work, and the chrome stays out of the way of the text. A rule that fails in one of the nine themes (light: Ledger, Gray, Cool, Harbor; dark: Aubergine, Graphite, Midnight, Obsidian, Indigo Night) fails here.

## Colour

- Theme tokens only: `--background`, `--card`, `--foreground`, `--muted`, `--muted-foreground`, `--accent` (the neutral wash of a current instrument well, not the orange accent of the bullets below), `--border`, `--hairline`, `--primary`, `--primary-foreground`, `--ring`, `--accent-ink`, `--accent-text`, `--tone-*`, `--stat-*` (the four token components of Settings' statistics, below), `--doc-1..8`. No colour literal, no `color-mix` that invents a hue, no gradient.
- The themes (`tokens-light.css`, `tokens-dark.css`): Ledger, light, stone paper with the amber `--primary` `oklch(0.84 0.155 80)`; Gray, light, a neutral paper at hue 270 with a near-black `--primary` `oklch(0.22 0.005 270)` that takes light text and a grey `--ring` `oklch(0.55 0.04 270)` (`:root[data-light-theme="gray"]:not(.dark)`); Cool, light, a blue-white paper at hue 235 with a near-black `--primary` `oklch(0.22 0.015 235)` that takes light text and a blue `--ring` `oklch(0.55 0.10 235)` (`cool`); Harbor, light, a cool blue-white at hue 235 with a teal `--primary` `oklch(0.46 0.09 205)` that takes light text (`harbor`); Gray and Cool are stored light variants the theme picker does not offer; Graphite, the dark default (`theme-default.ts` seeds it), a neutral charcoal at hue 270 (`.dark[data-dark-theme="gray"]`) with an orange `--primary` at hue 55; Aubergine, a purple dark at hue 325 with an amber `--primary` `oklch(0.83 0.165 82)` (`.dark[data-dark-theme="aubergine"]`), never the planner's default; and three dark themes with the amber `--primary` `oklch(0.82 0.15 85)` too: Midnight, a deep navy at hue 255 (`midnight`), Obsidian, a near-black OLED ground at hue 260 (`obsidian`), and Indigo Night, a blue-violet ink at hue 280 (`indigo`). Graphite and Obsidian surfaces (`--background`, `--card`, `--muted`, `--secondary`, `--accent`, `--border`, the sidebar tokens) keep chroma at or under 0.015, so on them the only saturated colours on a page are the accent and the status tones; Aubergine, Midnight and Indigo Night tint their surfaces on purpose (chroma 0.03 to 0.05). Measured (WCAG relative luminance from OKLCH), as foreground on background, muted foreground on card, accent on background and the text on the accent: Graphite 14.7:1, 6.6:1, 7.5:1 and 7.5:1; Midnight 15.6:1, 7.3:1, 10.9:1 and 10.7:1; Obsidian 16.4:1, 7.8:1, 11.4:1 and 11.3:1; Indigo Night 15.6:1, 7.3:1, 10.9:1 and 10.7:1; Harbor 15.2:1, 6.8:1, 5.9:1 and 6.4:1.
- The dark accent is orange, `oklch(0.74 0.15 55)`, for `--primary` and `--ring`, with `--primary-foreground` the theme's own background. The accent marks the primary button's fill, the focus ring, the selection and the arrival flash in the reading column, and the chosen option of a segmented choice, and nothing else. It is never a border, a tint behind a surface, a heading colour or a status.
- Contrast (WCAG relative luminance from the OKLCH tokens): text meets 4.5:1 (1.4.3) and a control's edge, a focus ring or a glyph that carries meaning meets 3:1 (1.4.11) against what it sits on, in every theme. `--accent-ink` (`index.css`: `--ring` on Ledger, `--primary` in the dark themes) is the token for accent-coloured lines and glyphs (the specimen's rule, the chosen size tile's edge and glyph, the live wrap-up dot), 3.5:1 on the Ledger page and 3.8:1 on its card, where the amber fill is 1.5:1. `--accent-text` (`index.css`: a deeper ochre `oklch(0.5 0.11 80)` on Ledger, `--primary` in the dark themes) is the token for an accent-coloured text run (New work's eyebrow, a pending tool row's target), 4.9:1 on the Ledger page, 5.3:1 on its card and 4.6:1 on `--muted`; in the dark themes both are the orange at 7.5:1 or more.
- `--tone-warning` is amber at hue 80 in every dark theme. Graphite's `--hazard-warning` is amber too, 25 degrees off its orange accent at hue 55, so a warning never reads as the accent; in Aubergine, Midnight, Obsidian and Indigo Night, whose accent is amber, `--hazard-warning` is orange (hue 55), and the warning tone is told from the accent by its glyph and word. Ledger's warning stays orange (hue 50) beside its amber accent. Every dark tone measures at least 3.4:1 on its theme's card (the lowest is Graphite's `--tone-dimmed`; `--tone-completed` is 8.6:1 there), every Harbor tone at least 4.5:1 on the Harbor card, and the Ledger tones at least 3.4:1 on the Ledger card. As every status, a warning is a glyph and a word, never a hue alone.
- `--tone-*` colours a glyph, never a text run, a border or a background; the Findings well's open state is an exception (Surfaces) and the diff's edges, washes and marks are the other (Diff view). Warnings use `--tone-warning`; `--destructive` is for an action that deletes.

## Type

Chrome is Alegreya Sans (`--font-sans`), chat prose included. The reading face (`--font-reading`) is set only on document text: the rendered body of a planning document (`.md`, `markdown/markdown.css`) and verbatim quotations of a document's text (a Coverage row's vision quote, a settle point's quote, a passage attached in chat, the stub's own words). Paths, ids, slugs and code keep `--font-mono`. Nothing is italic or old-style; figures are tabular. Sentence case everywhere; the `eyebrow` utility is the only uppercase.

| Token          | Size                            | Use                                                                                                                 |
| -------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--type-page`  | 20px/650, `.plan-page-title`    | the one title of a screen, under its eyebrow                                                                        |
| `--type-title` | 15px/600, `.plan-section-title` | a section inside a page, a panel head                                                                               |
| `--type-body`  | 14px/400                        | chrome prose: a sentence beside a button, a note, an empty state; the spine's rows and the chat's rows and controls |
| `--type-ui`    | 13px/500                        | buttons, rows, tabs, labels; in the spine and the chat, what is `.plan-fine` elsewhere                              |
| `--type-fine`  | 12px/400, `.plan-fine`          | a reason under a control, a count, a timestamp                                                                      |

The document step: a feature document's pages (its summary and source headings, Coverage and Findings) are read as documents, so their headings sit larger than `--type-page`, all in the chrome face (`--font-sans`) at 600 with -0.02em tracking. Alegreya Sans is a text face, drawn at 119% (`size-adjust`, `fonts.css`), and its default spacing opens up at these sizes, so -0.02em closes each heading into one word shape; it has no 600, so the 700 file serves 600 and the headings draw in its bold, a clear step above the 500 rows under them. The page title is 40px on a 46px line (30px on a 36px line under 30rem of heading width, `trace/trace-heading.css`; on Coverage and Findings 32px under a 480px viewport, `trace/maps.css`); a section header (a Coverage section, a Findings group) 24px; a finding title 22px (20px under 36rem of list width), a plan page's section heading and the settle confirmation's title 22px. Row headings under them are `--font-sans` at 500: a Coverage heading row 18px, a What it implements row 17px, a decided finding's title 16px; the finding titles in an open Coverage row are 17px at 600. The scope standfirst under a feature's title is the reading face at 1.1 × `--reading-size`; on Coverage and Findings the lede under the title is the chrome face at 1.1 × `--reading-size`, and a verbatim quote there (a Coverage row's vision quote) is the reading face at 0.9 × `--reading-size`. No title or heading uses the reading face. Every other screen keeps `--type-page`.

The two sidebars sit one step up the scale from the rest of the chrome, because they are read beside the reading column, not glanced at. In the spine (`feature/spine.css`): rows and the group names at `--type-body`; the mono ids and numbers, the counts, a section's state word and the "Source" line at `--type-ui`; every row leads with one 14px glyph (Status); the head's next-step link is one well with a `--type-body` row and a `--type-ui` reason (the spine's head, below). In the chat panel (`chat.css` root is `--type-body`): chrome (tool rows and fold line, notices, chip labels, context row, queue rows, permission band) at `--type-body`; the head title at `--type-title` (600; "New chat" at 500 in `--muted-foreground`) over its sub-line, the state word in `--foreground` and the place in `--muted-foreground`, at `--type-ui`; the empty state's title at 26px/500 and the left-behind title at 20px/500, both in `--font-sans`; user turns and the composer field at 15px (`--type-title`, turns at 500, field at 400); the assistant's prose at 16px/1.55 in `--font-sans`; secondary text (chip source, file size, timestamps, counts, `.plan-fine`, `size="sm"` buttons) and mono tool and permission bodies at `--type-ui`; the composer's foot line at `--type-fine`, the panel's one 12px text (Chat). The kickoff transcript inherits the chat's sizes. Full-size buttons in the chat are 14px; the hearth's shaped buttons (Chat) are 32px. Nothing else in either sidebar drops below 13px.

Hierarchy is made with size, weight and space, in that order. A surface uses the weights the scale above names (400, 500, 600, and 650 for the page title) and no others. Muted text (`--muted-foreground`) marks what is secondary; it never marks what is disabled.

## Space

`--space-1..7` = 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48px. Inside a control and between a glyph and its word: 1-2. Between rows: 2-3. Between a title and its content: 3. Between sections: 5-6, with no rule and no box between them. Page gutter: `--page-gutter` (24px, 16px below 480px). Reading text keeps its 68ch column; chrome prose stops at 48ch.

## Surfaces and borders

- The backend unavailable modal (`connection-dialog.tsx`) uses Aurora's warning `HazardPanel`: diagonal caution stripes stay in its header, with a clean body on the shared dialog surface. The shared overlay blurs the page by 6px. It stays open through reconnect attempts and closes when the socket opens; an initial connection attempt and graceful stopping keep their existing notices until a connection fails.
- Substantial failures and warnings use Aurora hazard surfaces: editor load failures and unreadable plan metadata use `ErrorPanel`; stale-document refresh failures use a warning `HazardPanel` with Retry. Editor and document validation group errors in one error panel and warning buckets in one warning panel, with notes outside. Stripes stay in the headers and body text uses normal ink; alert announcements, source-line jumps, stage links and save controls keep their behavior. Short inline action errors and ordinary loading and empty states retain their compact forms.

- The page ground is `--background`. One level of surface exists: `.plan-surface` (`--card`, hairline edge, 10px corners) standing on the ground. Nothing inside a surface draws a border, a shadow or another surface. A box in a box is not allowed; the Findings fold picker is the one recorded exception (Floating surfaces).
- A border is allowed around: an input, a surface standing alone on the page, a popover or dialog. A border is not allowed around: a button row, a list that sits on the page, a status, a heading, a notice.
- Lists divide with hairlines (`.plan-list`, `.plan-rule`); their edges are the page's edges. A hairline separates; it never encloses.
- Shadows exist only on things that float: popovers and dialogs (`--plan-shadow` under `--popover-lift` for a popover, `--dialog-shadow` for a modal; Floating surfaces gives the tokens, the halo and the raised fill). Nothing in the page flow casts one.

## Buttons

`DisabledReason` draws every button (`.plan-button`, 28px high, 13px/500, 6px corners, one line, `white-space: nowrap`). `OfferButton` wraps it for an offer. Three looks through `look`:

- `quiet`: no edge, muted text until hovered. Toolbar actions, Source and Edit, icon buttons.
- exception: the header's Chat button (`ChatSheetButton`, `chat/panel-collapse.tsx`) is a quiet `.plan-button` that is always shown on a chat route. From 1280px it is the side panel's disclosure: `aria-expanded` while the panel is open fills it `--muted` in `--foreground` (the `.plan-button` expanded state), and it is named "Collapse chat" or "Expand chat" with the state and counts. Below 1280px it opens the sheet. Its decision count is a 16px `--primary` pill and its attachment count a 16px `--muted` pill with a `--border` ring, both inside the 28px line box.
- `secondary`: hairline edge, transparent. Every offer and action by default.
- `primary`: `--primary` fill. At most one per screen, and only its owner passes `look="primary"`: the surface that draws `checklist.primary` (the pace card holding it on `product` and `metaplan` while pace shows, the queue row, or the page that owns it), a dialog's confirm (a dialog floats and owns its own), and the Start of the home and kickoff screens. `DisabledReason` is secondary unless told otherwise. The chat's own primaries, one per surface, are under Chat.

`shape` (`pill` or `round`, drawn as `data-shape`, `ui/hearth.css`) is the hearth's 32px full-radius variant, used only in the chat and on `/new` (Chat); it wins over `size="sm"`. `reasonId` names an element the caller draws the blocked reason in; the button is described by it and draws none of its own, so the reason is still drawn once.

`size="sm"` is the 24px/12px row variant for an action inside a list row, a panel head or the document heading (Page rhythm), where every control (Edit, Discuss and Open all in the toolbar bar, the Summary / Source switch's segments, Rebuild and Pressure-test at a status row's end) is a bare `.plan-button` of that size, quiet, on one 24px centre line; Pressure-test alone carries its flask and a resting `--muted` fill (a `--border` edge on a summary page's chip row), because beside a status word a bare quiet button reads as one more word. The heading draws no secondary and no primary button: the bar and the status card carry the edges, and the quiet buttons sit inside them. A feature document's summary page is the exception (Surfaces): its action row is 32px bordered buttons. A label is a verb plus at most two words (`Start`, `Move`, `Rebuild`), except that an offer's server label is drawn as sent (`Write plan feat-planner-01-documents`); a label carrying a document's slug truncates and never wraps. The sentence beside the button explains, so the button never carries the explanation. A screen never draws one offer twice: each offer has one owner, decided by its id.

Blocked: `aria-disabled`, 50% opacity, no hover change, and the reason beneath in `.plan-fine`, or after the button on its line in the document header's summary status line (`reasonPlacement="inline"`). The reason is the control's accessible description, so it is drawn once per blocked control and never repeated in a second line or a banner under the same control.

## Status

Status is a glyph and a word (`.plan-status`), inline with the text it describes: `✓ done`, `○ not written`, `· optional`, and the current item marked with `--primary`. ## Floating surfaces

What floats (a popover, a dialog, a menu, the selection pill, a sheet) stands one step up the surface ramp and is set off by three things: a raised fill, an edge and a halo. The tokens are in `src/index.css`, `plan/plan.css` and the two token sheets, one set per scheme across the nine themes (four light: Ledger, gray, cool, Harbor; five dark).

- `--popover` is the raised fill, one step above `--card` in every theme. Light: Ledger `oklch(0.985 0.005 102)` over a card of 0.955; gray `oklch(1 0 270)`, cool `oklch(1 0 235)` and Harbor `oklch(1 0 235)` over cards of 0.985 to 0.987. Dark: Aubergine `oklch(0.235 0.045 325)` over 0.195, Graphite `oklch(0.265 0.004 270)` over 0.23, Midnight `oklch(0.24 0.037 255)` over 0.2, Obsidian `oklch(0.2 0.01 260)` over 0.16, Indigo Night `oklch(0.24 0.05 280)` over 0.2. The dark themes lift about 0.04 L because a shadow does not show on near-black; the three near-white light themes can only lift to white, so there the edge and the halo carry the separation. Text stays AA on the fill: `--muted-foreground` 5.9:1 at the least (Graphite), every `--tone-*` 3.1:1 or more.
- The edge is `--popover-border`, `color-mix(in oklab, var(--foreground) 26%, var(--popover))`, mixed in oklab because oklch interpolates hue along the arc. The primitives draw it as their 1px ring (`ring-(--popover-border)`), and a rule forces it as `border-color` on `[data-slot="popover-content"]` and `[data-slot="dialog-content"]`, so a consumer that swaps the ring for `border border-border ring-0` (the activity popover, the pressure dialog) gets the same edge. The chat command list and the chat's passage popup draw it as a 1px `box-shadow` ring, since they have no border; the feature picker draws it the same way, and the selection pill and its note draw it as their 1px border. It measures 1.9:1 against the fill in all nine themes, 1.6 to 1.7:1 against a light page and 2.1 to 2.3:1 against a dark one. It is held under 3:1 on purpose: it is a hairline that separates beside the fill and the halo, and a 3:1 line (L 0.47 on Obsidian, 0.66 on Ledger) would outline every popover like a focus ring.
- The halo is `--popover-glow`, a blurred ring of the theme's own `--foreground`: light `0 0 48px -8px` at 18%, which reads as a shade on paper; dark `0 0 36px -4px` at 16%, which reads as a glow tinted by the theme (pink on Aubergine, blue on Midnight, violet on Indigo Night). A drop shadow is invisible on Obsidian (page L 0.13), so depth in the dark comes from light, and one token built from `--foreground` serves both schemes. The halo is listed first in `--plan-shadow` and `--dialog-shadow`, so it paints over the black drops that would otherwise cover it.
- The shadow tokens. `--plan-shadow` (`plan.css`) is the halo over a contact shadow and a near drop: light `0 1px 2px oklch(0.2 0.02 100 / 0.1), 0 8px 24px -4px oklch(0.2 0.02 100 / 0.14)`, dark `0 1px 2px oklch(0 0 0 / 0.5), 0 10px 30px -4px oklch(0 0 0 / 0.6)`. `--popover-lift` is the longer drop under it: light `0 22px 56px -10px oklch(0 0 0 / 0.24)`, dark `0 24px 64px -8px oklch(0 0 0 / 0.8)`. A popover, a menu and the selection pill always layer `var(--plan-shadow), var(--popover-lift)`. `--dialog-shadow` (`index.css`) is the halo over a tight contact shadow and a deep drop, light `0 12px 28px -12px oklch(0 0 0 / 0.3), 0 32px 80px -20px oklch(0 0 0 / 0.45)`, dark `0 16px 40px -4px oklch(0 0 0 / 0.85), 0 40px 100px -8px oklch(0 0 0)`; the modal's drop is too heavy under a small menu, so a popover takes the lighter pair. `--plan-shadow` and `--popover-lift` sit on `:root` and `.dark`, not on `[data-loom-plan]`, because a popover portals out of the shell.
- Backdrops. A modal dims and blurs the page: `--dialog-backdrop` is `color-mix(in oklch, var(--foreground) 30%, transparent)` in the light themes (the theme's ink, so the page dims without greying) and `oklch(0 0 0 / 0.58)` in the dark themes (ink would lighten a dark page), with `--dialog-backdrop-blur` at 6px on the dialog primitive's overlay (`components/ui/dialog.tsx`). The settle and chat sheets' overlays take `--dialog-backdrop` and no blur, since their markup is not the primitive's. A non-modal popover draws no scrim and no blur: (1) the page under it stays live, and a scrim that lets clicks through dims content that still works while one that takes clicks blocks the trigger's own toggle and the page; (2) the activity popover opens on its own when a question arrives, without taking focus, so a scrim would dim the page under someone typing in the composer; (3) the settle and plan-glyph popovers point at the text beside them, which a blur would hide. A popover is separated by its fill, edge and halo; Escape and an outside click are untouched.
- The raised-surface rule (`index.css`) applies to `[data-slot="popover-content"]`, `[data-slot="dialog-content"]`, `.terminal-frame`, `.trace-sheet`, `.chat-commands`, `.add-to-chat__pill`, `.add-to-chat__note`, `.kickoff-picker` and `.maps-picker`. Inside such a layer `--card` is `--popover` and `--background` is `--popover-well` (the page's `--card`), and the washes and rules are mixed in oklab from the layer's own fill: `--muted` and `--secondary` at `--foreground` 6%, `--accent` 10%, `--input` 14%, `--border` 16%, and `--hairline` at 85% of that `--border`. Consumers paint the layer `bg-card` or `var(--card)` (the activity popover, the pressure dialog, planner settings, the dashboard help) or `var(--background)` (the planner help, the tree drawer), so the raise reaches them without editing their markup; and the page's `--muted` is within 0.01 L of a dark `--popover`, so an unremapped hover wash would vanish on the raised fill. Text on the mixed washes: `--muted-foreground` 5.2:1 or more on `--muted` and 4.7:1 or more on `--accent` (Graphite, gray, cool). Known limit: `--tone-dimmed` on a hovered row is 2.7:1 on Graphite and 3.0:1 on Midnight and Indigo Night; revisit when that tone is lifted. A new floating surface uses a primitive or joins the selector list.
- exception: the Findings fold picker (`.maps-picker`, `trace/maps-findings-forms.css`) is a raised panel inside the Findings card, the one box in a box on the page (Surfaces and borders): `--popover` fill, a 1px `--popover-border` edge, `--corner-surface` corners and `--space-4` padding, and no shadow, because it sits in the page flow. Its chosen option is `color-mix(in oklab, var(--accent-ink) 16%, var(--popover))` with an inset 1px ring of `--accent-ink` at 45%, and hover is the layer's `--muted`; an option's corners are `--corner-control`. The tint differs from the card and from the panel in hue and lightness in all nine themes (the accent ink: amber on Ledger and four of the dark themes, orange on Graphite, grey-blue on gray, blue on cool, teal on Harbor), and the checked radio is the cue that is not colour. Text on the tint: `--foreground` 9.6:1 or more, `--muted-foreground` 4.58:1 or more (Graphite).

## Diff view

The diff (`diff/diff-view.tsx`, `rendered-diff.tsx`, `diff.css`) is one surface: a head over a Rendered body or a Source body, on the host's own ground. It draws no box, card or second surface of its own.

- exception: in a diff a tone colours more than a glyph (Colour). The insert colour is `--tone-completed` and the delete colour `--tone-blocked`, scoped on `.diff-view` as `--diff-add` and `--diff-del`, the two tones the chat's `+N −M` counts use for the same meaning. No hue is invented and no per-scheme literal added: the tones already differ by scheme (light `oklch(0.49 0.12 150)` and `oklch(0.52 0.18 25)`, dark `oklch(0.77 0.13 152)` and `oklch(0.72 0.16 22)`). A change is marked by an edge, a wash and a mark in its tone, because a diff's two sides have to be told apart at a glance; each side is doubled by its glyph (`+` or `−`) and a spoken label, so hue never stands alone.
- The washes are transparencies of the tone, so they sit on any host ground (the page `--background`, `--card`, a dialog's `--popover`): `--diff-add-wash` at 12% and `--diff-del-wash` at 10% in the four light themes, both at 14% in the five dark ones (`.dark .diff-view`). The changed run inside a changed source line is a second, firmer layer, `--diff-add-mark` and `--diff-del-mark` at 24%, with its text forced to `--foreground`. Measured from the OKLCH tokens in all nine themes on `--background`, `--card`, `--popover` and the raised dialog surface: `--foreground` on a wash 9.3:1 or more and on a mark 5.8:1 or more; a tone's 2px edge and its `+` or `−` on its own wash 4.0:1 or more (4.75:1 on the plain ground); a wash against its ground 1.16 to 1.33:1, so the wash never carries the meaning alone. One share per scheme holds every theme above AA, so no theme has its own value. The changed run is a fill and not an underline, which measured 2.98:1 on Ledger.
- Muted and syntax inks are deepened inside the diff: muted text on a wash falls to 4.46:1, and the editor's light-theme syntax inks (`--chart-1..4`, set in `codemirror.ts`) start at 2.7:1 to 5.0:1 on the plain page. `--diff-deepen` is `--foreground` at 45% in the light themes and 25% in the dark ones; `--diff-ink-muted` and `--diff-ink-1..4` mix the muted ink and the four chart inks toward it in oklab. They are re-pointed onto `--muted-foreground` for a rendered change's side (`.diff-side`) and onto `--muted-foreground` plus `--chart-1..4` for the source text (`.diff-source .cm-content`). The intermediate names keep a custom property from referring to itself. The worst case on a wash is 5.3:1 in the light themes (chart-2 on Ledger) and 5.4:1 in the dark (chart-3 on Aubergine). Line numbers keep the plain muted ink.
- Rendered reads as the document does: one column in the reading face (`--font-reading` at `--reading-size` and `--reading-line`; `diff.css` falls back to `plan.css`'s defaults because a dialog portals out of `[data-loom-plan]`). A change is marked in a 32px margin (`--diff-gutter`, `--space-6`): a 2px edge in the tone (a border, so it survives forced colours), a mono `+` or `−` in the gutter (hidden from assistive technology, baseline-aligned to the first line) and the wash. The removed text is a `del` and the added text an `ins`, each led by a screen-reader label ("Removed:", "Added:", "Changed from:", "Changed to:"). A run of added blocks, or of removed ones, joins into one band, and unchanged blocks carry no mark. The bands are square-ended and fill the reading column, like the source view's line washes; a heading inside a band keeps 0.9em of space above it. The head, the text and the fold rows share the gutter's inner edge.
- A changed block shows its text before and after: stacked as one band below 900px, side by side from 900px with a 12px gap. A replaced run of blocks pairs by word overlap (Dice 0.5 or more) in document order, so an edited paragraph sits beside its original and an inserted or deleted one stays added or removed; blocks between two similar pairs pair by position, and a run past 10,000 block pairs pairs by position throughout. The layout comes from `DiffView`'s one media query (`min-width: 900px`, the source view's own breakpoint), written to `data-diff-layout` on the root, so the rendered and source views switch together and CSS holds no second breakpoint. Added, removed and unchanged blocks stay in the one reading column at every width: only a changed block has two texts to compare, and an added text in a right column would break the flow of the unchanged text around it.
- The head is one line under a hairline, wrapping when it must: what changed in words at `--type-ui` in `--muted-foreground` ("New file" or "Deleted file" at 500 in `--foreground`, then the counts of added, removed and changed blocks with only the glyph toned, or "No changes"), and at its end the Rendered / Source switch. The switch is two flush segments in a `--border` track at `--control-height`, the pressed one filled `--muted` and set in `--foreground` at 600, its focus ring inset; a hidden copy of each label at 600 holds the widths, so pressing moves nothing. The head draws no path: the host prints it right above, and no two mono paths stand in a row (Do not). It is sticky at the top of the diff, over `--background` where the host's page scrolls (the diff page, the changes list under 1024px), and on the dialog's own surface inside a dialog, which bounds the diff so its body scrolls inside itself.
- A run of unchanged blocks away from a change folds into one row: a chevron in the gutter, "N unchanged blocks" at `--type-ui`/500 muted, then a hairline to the edge. The row is a button with `aria-expanded` that opens the run under itself and closes it again. Two unchanged blocks stay on each side of a change, a run that would hide fewer than four stays open, and a diff with no change shows the document whole. A plan document is hundreds of blocks with a handful of changes; the folds put the changes together and show context instead of a long scroll.
- Source is the CodeMirror diff of the text, loaded only when chosen. Its gutter takes the host's ground with no edge, its change bar is the rendered edge's 2px, its text is `--type-body` in the editor's mono face, and its two panes are divided by one hairline; below 900px it is unified. Its changed lines wear the same washes, and the changed run the same marks.
- On open the rendered view jumps (instant, so the same under reduced motion) to its first changed block: centred in what the sticky head leaves of the box it scrolls in when the block fits, else at its start under the larger of the head's height and the 48px scroll margin. The source view calls the editor handle's `scrollToFirstChange` once, on the first editor built; a rebuild (new text, a resize across 900px) does not scroll again.
- The rendered scroller (`.diff-rendered`) is a named group ("Rendered changes") with `tabindex="0"` and an inset focus ring, so the keyboard can scroll a diff that holds no link or fold row. Blocks and fold rows carry the 48px scroll margin, so keyboard focus never lands under the sticky head (WCAG 2.2, 2.4.11). The scroller is positioned, so the blocks' absolutely placed screen-reader labels scroll with it and do not stretch a dialog that holds the diff.

## Loading, empty and error states

A container waiting on data, holding nothing, or failing to load draws `LoadState` (`ui/load-state.tsx`, `load-state.css`) and nothing else: a glyph, a title at `--type-body`/500 in `--foreground`, an optional detail at `--type-ui` in `--muted-foreground` (drawn through `Untrusted`), and an optional action row (one secondary button, then links). It is centred on both axes in whatever holds it: it grows into a flex column's spare height and, as an only child, takes its parent's full height, so the parent must have a definite height (the shell's `main`; `.plan-layout`, a grid so its page area stretches; `.changes-page` and `.concept-unloaded` at `min-height: 100%`; the file mode columns at `min-h-full`). `size="compact"` tightens the padding for a popover, a card, a dialog section or a sidebar.

- Loading is the spinner (`ui/spinner.tsx`): a hairline ring at 18% of `currentColor` and one round-capped arc that turns (900ms) while its length breathes, at the state's icon size (40px on a page, 24px compact) on the page's own background. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` it does not turn and the arc's opacity eases instead. The state is `role="status"` named by its title (a muted 400), and fades in after 160ms so a load that answers at once never flashes.
- Empty (`Inbox`) and not found (`SearchX`) are the state's figure: a 40px muted icon at stroke 1.25 in a 72px `--muted` disc on a page, 24px in a 44px disc when compact; error (`CircleAlert`) takes `--tone-blocked` on the icon alone. An inline-glyph size (14 to 20px) is never a state's figure. No dashed box, no hazard stripes, no card.
- A busy line inside a control or after an action ("Sending…", "Uploading…") is `BusyText` (`ui/busy-text.tsx`): the 1em arrow spinner before the words (Status, rule 2), inside the caller's own role. A failed action's message is `ActionError` (`ui/action-error.tsx`), an alert line under the control, never `LoadState`.
- A sentence inside a list, a shelf or a partial page (an empty shelf's explanation, "No open questions", a concept page's empty-state note above its offers) stays `.plan-empty`: one muted sentence where the content would start. The home and kickoff doodles are the only illustrations.

## Focus and hover

- Focus: `outline: 2px solid var(--ring)` at 2px offset on every focusable thing, the same as the reading column, including the Settings theme cards (`components/theme-picker.tsx`, portalled outside `[data-loom-plan]`). No ring shadows, no inner glows. One sign per focused thing: a box around words (the chat composer, the kickoff field) draws the ring off its resting edge while the words have focus and never also turns its edge to `--ring`, which drew a second ring inside the first; a wrapper draws a `:focus-within` ring only over a control that draws none of its own (the pressure card's hidden select, the help search). `ui/focus-rings.test.ts` scans every planner sheet for an edge turned to the ring beside an outline or under `:focus-within`. The displaced rings: the feature picker's (New work), where a listbox shows its focus on its active option, inset; a Coverage tile's, inset because the tile band clips its corners (`trace/maps.css`); a settle menu item's, inset because the menu scrolls (`trace/trace-settle.css`); and a Coverage row's, drawn round the whole line by its toggle's `::after`.
- Hover: `--muted` wash on quiet and secondary buttons and on rows that open; the primary darkens through `color-mix` with its own foreground. Transitions are 120ms and off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- A link in chrome underlines on hover only; a link in reading prose is always underlined.

## Do not

Drawn from the screens before this system:

- Do not put a bordered button inside a bordered card, or a card inside a card grid's card.
- Do not repeat the banner's text under a blocked button (home's second `loom plan is in read-only mode` line): the reason line is enough.
- Do not make a label a sentence (`Move into a feature…`).
- Do not fill a button with `--primary` unless it is the screen's one primary.
- Do not tint a surface with the accent, or draw a gradient behind a card.
- Do not draw a six-segment coloured progress bar on a card; write the counts (Home's strip of step marks, under Surfaces, is glyphs beside a written count).
- Do not set two mono paths in a row under a title; one, truncated, is enough.
- Do not set a banner in two lines of toned text; one line, glyph toned, text in `--foreground`.
- Do not hatch or stripe a panel to show read-only; dim the control and state the reason.
- Do not cast a shadow on an in-page card or restyle a button from a wrapper (`[&_button]:`, `.x button`); pass `look`.
